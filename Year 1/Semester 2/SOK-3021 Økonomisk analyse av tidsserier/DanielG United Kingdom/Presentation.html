The ADF test results indicate that the unemployment rate is non-stationary, as the p-value is greater than 0.05. However, the GDP growth rate is stationary, with a p-value less than 0.05. After taking the first difference of the unemployment rate, it becomes stationary, as indicated by the significant p-value. Therefore, we conclude that the unemployment rate is integrated of order one I(1), while GDP growth is integrated of order zero I(0). We can now continue with the ARIMA modeling for the unemployment rate and cointegration analysis between the two variables.

ARIMA

The ARIMA model selected for the unemployment rate is ARIMA(1,1,1), which indicates that the series is differenced once to achieve stationarity, and includes one autoregressive term and one moving average term. The forecast for the next 8 quarters (2 years) shows a continuation of the small upward trend in unemployment, with the forecasted values gradually tapering off. The confidence intervals widen over time, reflecting increasing uncertainty in the forecasts. To evaluate whether including lagged values of economic growth improves forecast accuracy, we can fit a regression model with lagged GDP growth as an additional predictor and compare the forecast performance using metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE) or Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE).

ARIMA with lagged GDP growth

The ARIMAX model, which includes the lagged GDP growth as an exogenous variable, may provide improved forecasts if there is a significant relationship between economic growth and unemployment. By comparing the forecast accuracy of the ARIMA and ARIMAX models using MAE or RMSE, we can determine whether the inclusion of lagged GDP growth enhances the predictive performance for the unemployment rate. If the ARIMAX model shows a lower error metric compared to the ARIMA model, it would suggest that economic growth has a meaningful impact on forecasting unemployment. We include GDP growth lagged by one quarter because economic growth typically affects labor markets with a short delay, consistent with Okun’s law.

Given that we found no evidence of cointegration between unemployment and GDP growth, we will estimate a Vector Autoregression (VAR) model to explore the short-run dynamics between these two variables. The VAR model allows us to capture the interdependencies and feedback effects without imposing a long-run equilibrium relationship.

The VAR model estimation results indicate that the lagged values of both unemployment and GDP growth have significant effects on the current unemployment rate. Specifically, the first lag of unemployment has a positive and significant coefficient (1.3185), suggesting that higher unemployment in the previous quarter is associated with higher unemployment in the current quarter.

The first lag of GDP growth has a negative and significant coefficient (-0.0429), indicating that higher economic growth in the previous quarter is associated with lower unemployment in the current quarter, consistent with Okun’s Law.

Later lags of GDP or unemployment sometimes are not statistically significant, meaning their effect is weaker or negligible in the short run. The model explains a large portion of the variance in unemployment (R-squared = 0.9925), suggesting that past values of unemployment and GDP growth are strong predictors of current unemployment.

Conclusion

  • Unemployment rate in UK is non-stationary, while GDP growth rate is stationary.

  • Cointegration analysis indicates no long-run equilibrium relationship between unemployment and GDP growth.

  • The ARIMAX model with lagged GDP growth provides improved forecast accuracy compared to the ARIMA model alone.

  • The VAR model reveals significant short-run dynamics

  • Policymakers should consider the short-run interactions between unemployment and economic growth when designing economic policies aimed at reducing unemployment.

  • The ARIMA model for the unemployment rate indicates that it is non-stationary, while the GDP growth rate is stationary. The ARIMAX model, which includes lagged GDP growth, provides improved forecast accuracy compared to the ARIMA model alone, suggesting that economic growth has a significant impact on unemployment forecasts.

  • Cointegration analysis using the Engle-Granger method indicates that there is no long-run equilibrium relationship between unemployment and GDP growth, which is consistent with their different orders of integration.

  • The VAR model estimation reveals significant short-run dynamics between the two variables, with lagged unemployment positively affecting current unemployment and lagged GDP growth negatively affecting current unemployment, consistent with Okun’s Law.

  • Overall, the findings suggest that while there is no long-run relationship between unemployment and economic growth, there are significant short-run interactions that policymakers should consider when designing economic policies aimed at reducing unemployment.
