Volatility clustering

The plots display the log returns for the oil price, Aker BP, and Scatec. The data exhibits volatility clustering, characterized by periods of high variance alternating with periods of low variance. This clustering violates the assumption of constant variance. Time-varying conditional variance requires modeling via ARCH and GARCH specifications.

So its not stationary.

Can already see here that there are periods of volatility clustering so we can tell that the volatility is not constant over time.

Methodology

  • ARCH test

  • GARCH-modell

  • DCC-GARCH

  • DCC-GARCH T dist and norm

Arch test for the presence of ARCH effects, which would indicate time-varying volatility.

Garch model estimates the conditional variance for each series, capturing both the effect of past shocks and the persistence of volatility.

DCC-GARCH allows us to analyze the dynamic correlation between the oil price and stock returns, accounting for the time-varying nature of volatility.

“fat tails” We see excess kurtosis in the returns, so the extreme values are more likely than under a normal distribution. Therefore, we also estimate the DCC-GARCH model using a multivariate t-distribution to better capture the tail behavior of the returns.

Comparisons between the Multivariate Student-t (mvt) and Normal (mvnorm) distributions indicate that the t-distribution provides a superior fit. The
